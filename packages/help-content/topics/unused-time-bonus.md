## In short

Screen time {child} doesn't use by Sunday turns into pocket money.

## How to use it

1. Set the rate, for example 10p for every 30 minutes left over.
2. Set a weekly cap, so a quiet week has a limit.
3. It pays out on Sunday at 18:00, straight into {their} savings goal.

## Why it helps

It rewards using less screen time, instead of rewarding {them} with more of it. In a family trial, children had a weekly screen budget that came down over time, a small payment for each unused half hour, and praise from their parents. Their screen use fell by about 17 hours a week, and the drop lasted two years.

## What {child} sees

You saved 1 hour last week. That's 20p for your bike lights.

## Watch out for

Saving everything for one long weekend session. Keep the single-session limit on, and praise {their} choice to save, not just the money.

## The research

Epstein and colleagues, 2008, Archives of Pediatrics and Adolescent Medicine. 70 families with children aged 4 to 7. The screen budget was cut by 10% a month, and children were paid for each unused half hour. Screen use fell by about 17.5 hours a week at 6 months, against about 5.2 hours in the comparison group, and the reduction held at 24 months. The children were young, so treat the size of the effect as a guide for older children.
