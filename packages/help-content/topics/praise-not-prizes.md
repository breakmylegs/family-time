## In short

Some things only get praise, because {child} already loves doing them.

## How to use it

1. Mark a task as Praise only.
2. When {child} logs it, send a short message.
3. Say what {child} did, not just "well done".

## Why it helps

Paying children for things they already enjoy can make them enjoy those things less, because the activity starts to feel like a job. Praise doesn't carry that risk.

## What {child} sees

Mum says: The harbour looks amazing. Love the lighthouse.

## Watch out for

A surprise treat now and then is fine. Putting a price on LEGO or the trampoline is the thing to avoid.

## The research

Deci, Koestner and Ryan, 1999, Psychological Bulletin. Rewards that children expected for doing something they found interesting reduced how often they chose to do it later. The effect was stronger in children than adults. Unexpected rewards made no clear difference.
