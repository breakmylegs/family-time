## In short

{child} can add things {they} did away from screens, and things {they} are learning to do, on the kiosk under Me. You answer with praise, never points or money.

## How to use it

1. Look at Things {child} did on Family, and tap Send praise to say what you liked.
2. When {child} can really do something {they} were learning, tap Can do it now. It moves to Things I can do on the kiosk.
3. You can add something {child} is learning yourself, like riding a bike.

## How it works

Nothing here touches pocket money, points or time. A skill is marked only once a grown-up agrees {child} can do it, so it shows progress without a score. What {child} adds is only seen by {them} and by the grown-ups in the family, never by a brother or sister, as long as each child has a kiosk PIN when they share a tablet. The owner sets PINs in Settings, Kiosk. Each thing done counts towards the variety in the pilot measures.

## Why it helps

Paying children for things they already enjoy can make them enjoy those things less. Praise doesn't carry that risk.

## What {child} sees

What {they} added, your praise under it with your name, and the things {they} can do.

## Watch out for

Confirm a skill only when {child} can really do it. If you change your mind, tap Move back to learning.

## The research

Deci, Koestner and Ryan, 1999, Psychological Bulletin. Tangible rewards that children expected for doing something they found interesting reduced how often they chose to do it later. Praise and positive feedback did not.
