## In short

A short checklist of settings you change in the apps and on the devices themselves, chosen for {child}'s age. Family time can't change them for you, so you turn on a switch for each one once it is done.

## How to use it

1. Choose {child}'s age. The list changes to fit it.
2. Work through each item in the app or on the device it names.
3. Turn on the switch once it is done. Come back if an app changes its settings.

## How it works

Family time works on your home Wi-Fi. These settings work everywhere {child} takes the device, so the two together cover more than either alone. Family time doesn't check these settings. The switches are only a reminder, and they stay on your own server.

## Why it helps

Autoplay starts the next video before anyone chooses it. In one study of families with children aged 1 to 5, parents named it as a cause of battles over stopping. Screens at bedtime are linked to less sleep and poorer sleep, and even having a device in the bedroom, unused, is linked to poorer sleep. The UK's Chief Medical Officers advise screen-free mealtimes and phones outside the bedroom.

## Watch out for

Older children: agree settings together rather than set them alone. Rules made with reasons, recognising the child's view, are linked to children taking them on. Check in the first week that a game's own daily limit really stops play.

## The research

Hiniker and others, 2016: a diary study of families with young children, in which parents named autoplay as a driver of battles. Carter and others, 2016: a meta-analysis of cross-sectional studies, ages 6 to 19, linking bedtime device use to less and poorer sleep. UK Chief Medical Officers, 2019: expert guidance on mealtimes and bedrooms. Valkenburg and others, 2013: rules with reasons and the child's view linked to children taking them on.
