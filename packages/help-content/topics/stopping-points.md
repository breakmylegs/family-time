## In short

When a planned session, a request you said yes to, or an Allow runs to its end, Family time waits a few minutes before blocking, so {child} can reach a good place to stop: the end of a level, a video or a chat. The minutes don't come off {child}'s weekly time.

## How to use it

1. On the Family screen, under The whole family, find Time to find a stopping point. It starts when the pilot reaches the transitions phase.
2. Tap Change and pick Off, 5, 10 or 15 minutes. It starts at 5.
3. Talk to {child} about what a good stopping point is in the games {child} plays.

## Why it helps

In one study of children aged 1 to 5, routines and natural stopping points were linked to easier endings, and two-minute warnings were linked to more upset. A block that arrives mid-game is the kind of abrupt ending linked to upset.

## What {child} sees

On the kiosk: Finishing up. Find a good place to stop, like the end of a level, by about 19:05. There is no countdown. On Control you see the session's own end time, then Finishing up as the reason while the extra minutes run.

## Watch out for

It never runs into wind-down, bedtime or school time. It doesn't apply after you end something early with Normal or Undo, or after you used Block or Pause on it while it ran, and Pause and Block still start within a minute. If {child} starts something new in the extra minutes, talk about it at the family review, or turn it off for a while.

## The research

Hiniker and colleagues, 2016, CHI. In a diary study of families with children aged 1 to 5, routines and natural stopping points were linked to easier endings. Two-minute warnings were linked to more upset, and parents named autoplay as a driver of battles. It is an early finding in younger children, so watch how endings go for {child} using How did it end?
